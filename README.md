# ARMADA — a playable paper

**▶ Play now: https://lgs3.github.io/armada-play/** (phone-first; works on desktop)

**A thousand ships you can't touch, and six you can send.**

ARMADA is a one-screen game about *Contesting the PRC's Fishing Armada* (J.R. Williamson, DARC, May 2025). You run Peru's maritime response for a season. Past the 200-mile line sits a swarm of Chinese squid jiggers, fed by a handful of refrigerated motherships that carry the catch home. Your cutters can push the swarm around, but they can't remove it. Build evidence against the motherships, get them banned and listed, and when the US Congress authorises Letters of Marque, post bounties so privateers can take them. Every bounty costs standing with Beijing.

You can't win by force. You can survive with fish left. The game ends where the paper does: the realistic goal is a negotiated cap on the fleet, not endless seizure.

## How to play
- **Inside the line, draw.** A line from a port sends a cutter to board boats; a loop that crosses itself is a standing patrol.
- **Past the line, tap.** The runway launches a patrol plane; tap a mothership to send the helicopter to watch it. Evidence becomes a record when the aircraft lands.
- **Climb the ladder.** Records lead to a notice, a port ban, and finally a listing that privateers can act on.
- **Watch the meters:** your fish stock, the fleet's profit, your budget, and Relations with Beijing.

## The levels
| # | Level | What it teaches |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Line | Cutters move the fleet. They don't remove it. |
| 2 | Follow the Catch | The catch leaves your water for ships you can't board. |
| 3 | Paperwork | A ban moves a ship. Its sister takes its place. |
| 4 | The Armada | Enforcement alone collapses. |
| 5 | Letters of Marque | Letters don't win. They let you survive. |
| 6 | Pressure | Every bounty spends standing. Pace them. |
| 7 | Command | Beijing offers a cap. Accept it, or play on. |
| 8 | The Real Scale | An animation of the fleet at real scale, and the patrol ships it would take just to keep it in sight. |

## What it argues
1. A coastal state can't police a swarm boat by boat.
2. The refrigerated motherships are the critical node.
3. The law stops at the 200-mile line; past it, only the flag state may board.
4. Seeing a ship isn't proving anything; cases need evidence.
5. Port bans work only if every port joins.
6. Letters of Marque are the paper's middle path, and its shakiest part.
7. Every escalation has a price.
8. Much of the fleet is built for two jobs, civilian and military.
9. The aim is a cap, not endless seizure.

## What it simplifies
Most squid fishing past the line is unregulated rather than illegal, and most states would treat privateer seizures as piracy. Seized crews are often trafficked workers. The paper doesn't price privateers or say who oversees them. Fleet size is disputed: 2,551 vessels by China's own count (2023), up to ~17,000 by outside estimate (ODI 2020). The game is a model of the paper's argument, not an endorsement of it.

## Facts and sources
Every in-game fact and event card names its real-world source, and the in-game **Notebook** keeps the ones you've met. Key sources include the paper itself, Global Fishing Watch, SeafoodSource, the Overseas Development Institute, the US Department of Defense's 2024 China report, and the US Naval War College's China Maritime Studies Institute.

## About this repository
This repository holds only the built game: a single self-contained `index.html` with no tracking and no network calls. Progress is saved in your browser. It is published from a private source repository.
